# Conversion doctrine

## Core hierarchy

Build campaign strategy in this order:

1. target customer and life situation;
2. expensive problem, fear or blocked decision;
3. desired transformation;
4. product or construction capacity offered;
5. concrete offer and conversion event;
6. differentiating mechanism;
7. proof, reference, guarantee and risk reversal;
8. objection handling;
9. urgency or reason to act;
10. brand-appropriate CTA.

Do not start from an isolated feature found in a content source. "Well-used floor area" can prove a house plan is thoughtful. It is usually not the main reason a family buys a house.

## Residential priority stack

Use the brand's verified hierarchy, but normally evaluate these high-value purchase drivers before minor features:

- safety and predictability;
- fixed or calculable price;
- fixed or calculable deadline;
- verified quality and technical content;
- competitive total value;
- fast and organized construction;
- reliable prefabricated technology;
- typehouse choice and adaptation;
- built references and operating history;
- engineering competence;
- enforceable guarantees;
- distinctive market offer;
- financing or budget fit;
- confidence that the family can complete the project.

Use minor benefits only as supporting proof unless research or the brand strategy shows they are a primary purchase driver.

## Product-led program

For brands selling family houses or typehouses:

- make verified houses, plans, prices, build times and life situations the recurring heroes;
- keep at least 60% of the active campaign program product-led;
- use one-house ads, house-family carousels, life-stage collections and talking-house concepts;
- show the complete house without accidental cropping;
- pair name, gross floor area, valid price and build time as one understandable information unit when commercial data is allowed;
- use consultation, plot review and quotation as conversion mechanisms, not as every brand's core idea.

## Direct-response test

Reject copy unless a cold prospect can answer in seconds:

- What is being offered?
- Is it for someone like me?
- What problem does it solve?
- What is unusually strong about this brand or offer?
- Why should I believe it?
- What should I do next?

Reject concepts that sound intelligent but do not create desire, confidence or action.

## Hungarian quality test

Require:

- natural spoken word order;
- concrete nouns and verbs;
- short, decisive sentences;
- correct causal relationships;
- no literal translation patterns;
- no unexplained abbreviations or internal process language;
- no slogan whose meaning requires an explanation;
- no CTA that a customer would never naturally say.

Read every headline and CTA aloud. If it sounds like software, a legal memo or a 1980s brochure, fail it.
