# Brand separation

## Principle

Treat every brand as an independent company competing for attention. Shared ownership, technology or operational services do not permit shared advertising identity.

## Required campaign lane

Assign each campaign a unique lane containing:

- target segment;
- life situation;
- primary fear or desire;
- primary product or service;
- offer mechanism;
- proof stack;
- tone;
- narrative sequence;
- visual archetype;
- CTA.

No other active brand may use the same combined lane in the same campaign period.

## Shared claims

These are commonly available and therefore weak differentiators by themselves:

- plot review;
- engineer consultation;
- free quotation;
- fixed price;
- fixed deadline;
- high quality;
- fast construction;
- good value;
- transparent process;
- helpful team.

Use them as proof or support. They may become a primary message only when attached to a brand-exclusive offer, mechanism, evidence or life situation.

## Structural similarity

Treat creatives as duplicates when they reuse the same:

- large left or right message panel;
- headline-support-CTA geometry;
- three-card problem/process/CTA sequence;
- negative imperative headline formula;
- consultation-first funnel;
- stock family plus generic house composition;
- CTA with only a brand-name change.

Changing colors, fonts or photographs does not resolve structural duplication.

## Residential archetypes

Select different archetype mixes per brand, for example:

- verified typehouse product card;
- talking house with first-person personality;
- life-stage selector;
- price and deadline challenge;
- guarantee/proof stack;
- reference-led documentary;
- technology demonstration;
- design/editorial aspiration;
- renovation transformation;
- B2B project evidence;
- calculator or comparison tool;
- objection-led myth correction.

Record the selected archetype in the cross-brand registry and block reuse unless a human brand director explicitly approves the exception.
